# Product

Product management and discovery: how to decide what to build, work out what a piece of work actually is, validate problems, and balance exploration against exploitation.

## Notes

- [Predictability vs Outcomes](predictability-vs-outcomes.md) — Marty Cagan retracting his own *outcomes over outputs* framing, which this repo asserts in three places: output is a **means**, so opposing it to outcomes is a category error, and the real competitor is **predictability**. The mechanism is structural rather than attitudinal — feature teams set up *"to serve the business"* cannot own outcomes, so predictability is all they have left to promise. Carries Kniberg's *"100% predictability = 0% innovation"*, his own concession that predictability is sometimes genuinely required, and the reason the repo's accumulated AI throughput figures feel beside the point: they are all output measures, and output was never the variable in tension *(no evidence of any kind; SVPG sells the alternative, and the SAFe passage is assertion)*
- [The Four Abstraction Levels of Refinement](four-abstraction-levels-of-refinement.md) — why refinement fails when problem, functional solution, technical steps and work slicing are discussed at once; the linear-first-pass remedy, "User Fairytales" (stories reverse-engineered from implementation details), the preparation paradox, and Gall's Law applied to the first milestone
- [Exploring vs Exploiting in Product Discovery](explore-vs-exploit.md) — the two modes of discovery (divergent exploration vs convergent exploitation), when to switch, and the local-maxima risk of over-exploiting
- [Prioritisation as a Symptom](prioritisation-as-a-symptom.md) — the argument that prioritisation difficulty is an artefact of coupling and slowness rather than a skill to improve: speed and stable domain ownership as the structural substitutes, and why the approach relocates the trade-off rather than removing it
- [Modelling Software's Business Impact](modelling-business-impact.md) — the value side of a knowledge base otherwise full of activity metrics: NPV models over five categories, Pirate Metrics as the proxy layer for an unsolvable attribution problem, the organisational route to adoption (CEO/CFO sponsorship, finance as ally), and the argument that once AI relieves the engineering constraint it moves to **customer absorption capacity** — the same shape as CATS pointed at the customer rather than the codebase
